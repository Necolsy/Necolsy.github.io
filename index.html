01 / SHORT BIO

A researcher who
crosses boundaries.

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of the Built Environment at the National University of Singapore, working at the intersection of environmental fluid mechanics, experiments, and data-driven modelling.

My doctoral research asks how building geometry and porosity shape street-canyon ventilation. I designed and conducted pressure-tap wind-tunnel experiments and use RANS/LES simulations to examine pedestrian wind, air exchange, and model fidelity.

My route here began with civil and environmental engineering at the Technion, followed by an MSc at KAIST combining water-treatment experiments, operational data, and machine learning. I developed graph-attention and recurrent models for dosage and turbidity prediction, leading to first-author papers in Water Research and the Journal of Water Process Engineering.

At the Berkeley Education Alliance for Research in Singapore, I worked on infrared-camera data collection, image preprocessing, and urban thermal analytics. That work contributed to a first-author dataset publication in Scientific Data.

NOW / A*STAR

06 / PUBLICATIONS

Selected publications

Experiments, models, and data that make environmental systems easier to understand.

  1. JWEIA
    Design trade-offs in building porosity: A parametric analysis of vertical placement and geometry for urban ventilation
    Subin Lin, Jason Leong, and Hee Joo Poh
    Journal of Wind Engineering and Industrial Aerodynamics, 2026

    A validated RANS parametric study shows that the vertical location of a porous opening matters more than its size: ground-level porosity preserves pedestrian wind, while elevated voids can suppress it.

    DOI
  2. Sci. Data
    District-scale surface temperatures generated from high-resolution longitudinal thermal infrared images
    Subin Lin, V. Ramani, M. Martin, P. Arjunan, A. Chong, F. Biljecki, M. Ignatius, K. Poolla, and C. Miller
    Scientific Data, 2023

    A rooftop infrared observatory in Singapore produced 1.36 million district-scale thermal images, enabling fine-grained study of buildings, roads, and vegetation over time.

    DOI
  3. Water Res.
    Coagulant dosage determination using deep learning-based graph attention multivariate time series forecasting model
    Subin Lin, J. Kim, C. Hua, M. H. Park, and S. Kang
    Water Research, 2023

    GAMTF combines graph attention with time-series forecasting to jointly predict coagulant dosage and settled-water turbidity from long-term treatment-plant data.

    DOI
  4. JWPE
    Comparing artificial and deep neural network models for prediction of coagulant amount and settled water turbidity: Lessons learned from big data in water treatment operations
    Subin Lin, J. Kim, C. Hua, S. Kang, and M. H. Park
    Journal of Water Process Engineering, 2023

    Six years of operations data reveal why time-aware deep models improve predictions of both coagulant dosage and turbidity in a changing water-treatment process.

    DOI
  5. IAQVEC
    Aerodynamic response and ventilation enhancement in urban street canyons through building porosity: A comparative numerical and experimental study
    Subin Lin, Jason Leong, Boo Cheong Khoo, and Hee Joo Poh
    In E3S Web of Conferences, 2026

    Wind-tunnel measurements and validated RANS simulations compare three void-deck geometries, showing that a slot reaching toward the ground is most effective at flushing the pedestrian zone.
