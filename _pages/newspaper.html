Profile

Making sense of air, data, and cities.

Subin LinPhD Candidate · National University of Singapore

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of the Built Environment at the National University of Singapore, working at the intersection of environmental fluid mechanics, experiments, and data-driven modelling.

My doctoral research asks how building geometry and porosity shape street-canyon ventilation. I designed and conducted pressure-tap wind-tunnel experiments and use RANS/LES simulations to examine pedestrian wind, air exchange, and model fidelity.

Section BThe Urban Wind TimesResearch
Research interestsThree ways of asking better environmental questionsB1

Measure. Model. Make connections.

Three complementary ways of asking better environmental questions.

Urban fluid mechanics

Air moves. Geometry matters.

Building porosity, opening configuration, and array length can change pedestrian wind and canyon-scale exchange in different ways. I study those trade-offs through validated CFD.

Hands-on experiments

From the lab to the model.

Wind-tunnel models, pressure taps, tubing, data acquisition, and uncertainty analysis. Earlier work also brought me into water-treatment wet labs and infrared field sensing.

Environmental AI

Learning with physical context.

Graph learning and time-series forecasting for environmental operations; LLM-generated heuristics for building energy and HVAC diagnostics; emerging work on pollutant-source inference.
